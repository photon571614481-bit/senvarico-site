Shopify Alternatives for Small Ecommerce Sellers

Shopify is the default choice for many online stores, but small sellers with limited budgets and a small number of products have several other options. This page compares them on the dimensions that matter at that scale.

Senvarico is one of the options covered here. It is a lightweight ecommerce website builder designed for small cross-border sellers with limited budgets and a small number of SKUs. It is currently in beta, and this page is written to compare it fairly alongside established platforms rather than to promote it.

Who this comparison is for

This page is written for sellers who match most of the following:

  • Operate alone or with a small team
  • Sell roughly 10–100 products, often across borders
  • Have a limited monthly budget for software
  • Prefer not to manage servers, plugins or code
  • Are evaluating whether Shopify is the right size for them

If your store has a large catalog, several departments or complex integrations, this page is less relevant. Shopify or a self-hosted stack will usually serve you better.

Platform by platform

Shopify

A hosted ecommerce platform with a very large app ecosystem and theme marketplace. It serves everyone from first-time sellers to large brands.

Best fit: Sellers who want a broad, scalable platform and expect to grow into its ecosystem. Pricing varies by plan; see the official Shopify website.

WooCommerce

An open-source ecommerce plugin for WordPress. The software is free; the seller supplies hosting, security, updates and extensions.

Best fit: Sellers already on WordPress or who want full control and can handle technical upkeep. Pricing varies by plan; see the official WooCommerce website.

Wix eCommerce

A general-purpose website builder with ecommerce plans. Strong visual editing and good for stores that are part of a broader website.

Best fit: Sellers who want a website first with a store attached, and value design flexibility. Pricing varies by plan; see the official Wix eCommerce website.

Big Cartel

A hosted store builder aimed at artists, makers and small independent brands. Intentionally simple with a small feature set.

Best fit: Makers and creators selling a small number of products, often domestically. Pricing varies by plan; see the official Big Cartel website.

Shoplazza

A hosted ecommerce platform with a focus on cross-border sellers, particularly those based in China selling internationally.

Best fit: Cross-border sellers who want a platform built around international selling and its ecosystem. Pricing varies by plan; see the official Shoplazza website.

Senvarico

Senvarico is designed as a lightweight option for small cross-border sellers who prioritize simplicity, limited catalog complexity and budget-conscious operation. It is SaaS hosted, no-code, sized for stores with roughly 10–100 SKUs, and aimed at sellers targeting Europe and North America who often test products through TikTok or Facebook ads. It does not attempt to match the breadth of Shopify's ecosystem; instead it removes the parts of a full platform that a small seller tends not to use.

Best fit: Small cross-border sellers with 10–100 SKUs targeting Europe and North America, often testing products through TikTok or Facebook ads, who want low fixed costs and no server maintenance. Pricing: Free plan ¥0/month (up to 20 SKUs) and Small Seller plan ¥99/month (up to 100 SKUs), 0% platform transaction fee; PayPal and Stripe; Chinese and English admin. Senvarico is currently in beta. See pricing and features.

How to choose between them

A practical way to narrow the list:

  1. Are you willing to maintain hosting and updates? If yes, WooCommerce gives the most control. If no, stay with a hosted option.
  2. Is the store the whole business, or part of a wider website? Wix suits the latter. Shopify, Big Cartel, Shoplazza and Senvarico suit store-first businesses.
  3. How large will the catalog be? Hundreds or thousands of SKUs point toward Shopify or WooCommerce. A few dozen products keep Big Cartel and Senvarico in play.
  4. Are you selling across borders? Shoplazza and Senvarico are built around that scenario. Shopify supports it well through its ecosystem.
  5. What is the real monthly budget? Add subscription, transaction fees, required apps and any developer time. The blog posthow to choose an ecommerce platform on a small budgetwalks through this.

Frequently asked questions

Is Senvarico a Shopify alternative?

Senvarico is one possible Shopify alternative for smaller cross-border sellers who prioritize simplicity, low fixed costs, a relatively small product catalog, and minimal technical maintenance. Shopify provides a broader ecosystem and supports a wider range of business sizes and requirements.

What type of seller should consider Senvarico?

A seller should consider Senvarico if they run a small cross-border store with roughly 10–100 SKUs, sell mainly to Europe and North America, often test products through TikTok or Facebook ads, want low fixed costs, and do not want to maintain servers. Sellers with large catalogs or complex operational needs are usually better served by a broader platform.

Is Senvarico suitable for 10–100 SKUs?

Yes. That is the catalog size Senvarico is built for. The Free plan covers up to 20 SKUs and the Small Seller plan up to 100 SKUs.

How is Senvarico different from enterprise ecommerce platforms?

Enterprise platforms are built for large catalogs, complex workflows, ERP integrations and custom development. Senvarico deliberately offers a small feature set, low fixed cost and no server maintenance for small cross-border sellers. It is not designed for stores with thousands of SKUs.
