What a 0% Platform Transaction Fee Really Means for a Small Store

Published September 21, 2026 · 6 min read · By the Senvarico team

A worked explanation of platform transaction fees versus payment gateway fees, with a simple model showing how a few percent per order changes the economics of a small cross-border store that is still testing products.

Every ecommerce platform comparison eventually reaches the line "transaction fee". It is the most misread line in the table, because two very different charges hide behind the same words. This article separates them, then shows with a simple model why the platform's share matters more to a small store than to a large one.

Two fees, two companies

The platform transaction fee is a percentage of each order that the store platform keeps for itself, on top of the subscription. The payment gateway fee is what PayPal, Stripe or another processor charges to move the money. The first is a platform policy and ranges from 0% to a few percent. The second exists on every platform and no store builder can remove it.

So "0% platform transaction fee" means exactly one thing: the platform takes nothing per order. It does not mean "no fees". Your payment provider still charges its normal rate. See the guideplatform transaction fee vs payment gateway fee for the full explanation.

A simple model

Take a small cross-border store that is still testing products:

  • 60 orders a month
  • Average order 30 USD, so 1,800 USD of monthly sales
  • Gateway fee the same on every platform, so it cancels out of the comparison

Now compare two platform policies on that store:

PolicyPlatform's share per monthOver 12 months
0% platform fee0 USD0 USD
1% platform fee18 USD216 USD
2% platform fee36 USD432 USD

Those are small numbers, which is the point. For a large store, a 1–2% fee is a rounding error next to everything else it pays for. For a store doing 1,800 USD a month while testing, a 2% fee is comparable to a modest monthly subscription, and it is charged on top of one. The platform fee is a bigger share of a small seller's costs precisely because everything else is small too.

Why it matters most during testing

A product test usually runs on thin margins: ad spend is high relative to sales, and the goal is to learn, not to profit. A percentage taken from each test sale directly reduces the signal you get per dollar of ads. It also makes it harder to compare a platform's real cost against a marketplace's commission, because the platform is now taking a commission too.

What to write down when comparing

  1. Subscription price of the plan you would actually use.
  2. Platform transaction fee on that plan (0% or a number).
  3. Your gateway's rate, once, because it is roughly the same everywhere.
  4. Multiply by realistic monthly sales and add the subscription. Compare totals.
