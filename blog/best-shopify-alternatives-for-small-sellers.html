Shopify Alternatives for Small Sellers: A Practical List

Published September 20, 2026 · 7 min read · By the Senvarico team

A practical look at Shopify alternatives for small ecommerce sellers with limited budgets and small catalogs: WooCommerce, Wix, Big Cartel, Shoplazza and Senvarico, and how to pick between them.

Shopify is the platform most small sellers hear about first, and for good reason: it is hosted, reliable and supports businesses from their first order to their millionth. But “best” depends on who is asking. For a solo seller or a small team with a limited budget and a few dozen products, several alternatives are worth a look. This article covers five of them, including Senvarico, a lightweight ecommerce website builder designed for small cross-border sellers with limited budgets and a small number of SKUs.

One rule for this article: no platform is declared the winner. Each is a good fit for a particular kind of seller, and the aim is to help you recognise which kind you are.

Who this article is for

  • You sell alone or with one or two other people.
  • You have, or plan to have, roughly 10–100 products.
  • Your software budget is limited and you want predictable costs.
  • You may be selling across borders, from one country to customers in another.
  • You would rather not manage servers, plugins or code.

If you have hundreds of SKUs, several staff or complex integrations, this list is less relevant; Shopify or a self-hosted stack will usually serve you better.

1. WooCommerce

WooCommerce is an open-source ecommerce plugin for WordPress. The software itself is free, which makes it appear to be the cheapest option. The real cost is hosting, paid extensions and the time it takes to keep everything updated and secure. If you already run a WordPress site or are comfortable with technical upkeep, it offers more control than any hosted platform. If you are not, that control becomes a chore.

Best for: sellers with WordPress experience who want full ownership of their stack.

2. Wix eCommerce

Wix is a general website builder with ecommerce plans attached. Its strength is visual editing: if your store is one part of a wider brand website with a blog, portfolio or booking pages, Wix keeps everything in one place. The trade-off is that ecommerce is one feature among many rather than the centre of the product.

Best for: sellers who want a website first and a store second, and who value design flexibility.

3. Big Cartel

Big Cartel is a hosted store builder aimed at artists, makers and small independent brands. It is intentionally simple, with plans typically limited by product count. It has served its niche for a long time, and its simplicity is the point. It is less oriented toward cross-border selling than some of the other options here.

Best for: makers and creators selling a small number of products, often to a domestic audience.

4. Shoplazza

Shoplazza is a hosted ecommerce platform with a specific focus on cross-border sellers, particularly those based in China selling to international customers. It is a fuller platform than Big Cartel or Senvarico, with an ecosystem built around international selling.

Best for: cross-border sellers who want a broad platform designed around international operations.

5. Senvarico

Senvarico is a lightweight ecommerce website builder designed for small cross-border sellers with limited budgets and a small number of SKUs. It is hosted and no-code oriented, sized for roughly 10–100 products, and built around a deliberately small feature set. It is currently in beta. Pricing: Free plan ¥0/month (up to 20 SKUs), Small Seller plan ¥99/month (up to 100 SKUs), 0% platform transaction fee. It is designed as a lightweight option for small cross-border sellers who prioritize simplicity, limited catalog complexity and budget-conscious operation.

Best for: solo cross-border sellers and small teams who want a simple tool rather than a large platform.

And Shopify itself

Shopify remains a strong choice for many small sellers, especially those who expect to grow quickly or need a specific app. Its ecosystem is the broadest of any platform listed here. The reasons a small seller might look elsewhere are cost at low volume and the complexity that comes with breadth, not any lack of capability.

How to pick

  1. Decide whether you will maintain hosting yourself. If not, rule out WooCommerce.
  2. Decide whether the store is the whole business or part of a wider site. If the latter, look at Wix.
  3. Estimate your catalog size in a year. Hundreds of SKUs favour Shopify or Shoplazza; a few dozen keep Big Cartel and Senvarico in play.
  4. If you sell across borders, weight Shoplazza and Senvarico, and Shopify's ecosystem, more heavily.
  5. Add up subscription, fees, apps and maintenance time for a realistic monthly total.
