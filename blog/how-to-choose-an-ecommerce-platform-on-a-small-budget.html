How to Choose an Ecommerce Platform on a Small Budget

Published September 20, 2026 · 6 min read · By the Senvarico team

A step-by-step method for small sellers to compare ecommerce platforms by total cost, catalog size, maintenance burden and cross-border needs, rather than by headline price.

Most comparisons of ecommerce platforms start with the monthly price. For a small seller, that is the wrong starting point. The right one is the total cost of running your store for a year, in money and in hours. This article gives a simple method for working that out and using it to choose. It is published by Senvarico, a lightweight ecommerce website builder for small cross-border sellers, but the method applies to any platform.

Step 1: Describe your store honestly

Write down four numbers and one answer:

  • SKUs: how many products and variants you will list in the next twelve months.
  • Orders: a realistic monthly order count, not a hoped-for one.
  • Markets: how many countries you will sell to.
  • People: how many people will work in the store admin.
  • Maintenance: are you willing to manage hosting, updates and security yourself?

A seller with 30 SKUs, 40 orders a month, two markets, one person and no appetite for maintenance has a very different ideal platform from one with 800 SKUs and a part-time developer.

Step 2: Count all four costs

For each platform you are considering, estimate:

  1. Subscription. The plan that actually includes what you need, not the cheapest advertised tier.
  2. Transaction fees. Any platform fee on top of the payment processor's own fee, multiplied by your order estimate.
  3. Apps and extensions. Anything you will have to add to get basic selling functions working.
  4. Maintenance. Hosting and, honestly priced, your own hours or a developer's.

Add them up for twelve months. The result often reorders the list. Free software with hosting and upkeep can cost more than a modest hosted plan. A cheap base tier plus four apps can cost more than a mid tier that includes them.

Step 3: Check the fit, not just the price

A platform that is cheap but wrong-sized will cost you in other ways:

  • Too large: more settings, longer learning, features you pay for and never use.
  • Too small: you outgrow it and migrate, which costs time and sometimes SEO.

Match the platform to the numbers from step 1. Broad platforms such as Shopify fit almost any size but carry weight at the smallest end. Lightweight tools such as Big Cartel or Senvarico fit small catalogs and small teams and are wrong for large ones. Self-hosted options such as WooCommerce fit sellers who want control and can maintain it.

Step 4: Weight your special constraints

Two constraints change the answer for many small sellers:

  • Cross-border selling. If you sell from one country into others, currency display and shipping regions need to be simple. Platforms built around cross-border sellers, such as Shoplazza or Senvarico, treat this as core; on others it may need apps.
  • Product testing. If you are validating products rather than scaling a proven line, a low fixed cost matters more than a low percentage fee, because your volume is uncertain.

Step 5: Decide, and set a review date

Pick the platform with the best combination of total cost and fit for the store you described, then put a review date in the calendar six or twelve months out. Small stores change. The right platform for testing ten products is not always the right one for selling two hundred, and that is fine. Choosing for the stage you are in, not the stage you hope for, is what keeps a small budget intact.
