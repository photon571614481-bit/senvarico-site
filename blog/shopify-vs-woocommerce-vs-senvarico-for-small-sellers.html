Shopify vs WooCommerce vs Senvarico for Small Sellers

Published September 20, 2026 · 6 min read · By the Senvarico team

Three different approaches to ecommerce for small sellers: a broad hosted platform, a self-hosted open-source plugin, and a lightweight hosted tool. Which fits which seller.

Shopify, WooCommerce and Senvarico represent three different answers to the same question: how should a small seller run an online store? Shopify is a broad hosted platform. WooCommerce is a self-hosted open-source plugin. Senvarico is a lightweight hosted tool designed for small cross-border sellers with limited budgets and a small number of SKUs. This article compares the three approaches for small sellers without declaring a winner, because each is the right choice for a different seller.

The three approaches in one paragraph each

Shopify: broad and hosted

Shopify hosts everything, offers a large theme and app ecosystem, and scales from a first store to a large brand. A small seller gets reliability and room to grow. The cost of that breadth is a total monthly figure that can climb with apps and fees, and an admin with more options than a small store needs.

WooCommerce: flexible and self-hosted

WooCommerce turns a WordPress site into a store. The plugin is free and can be extended almost without limit. The seller, or someone they pay, is responsible for hosting, updates, backups and security. For a technical seller it is the most controllable option. For a non-technical one it is the most demanding.

Senvarico: lightweight and hosted

Senvarico removes most of what a small seller does not use. It is hosted, no-code oriented, sized for roughly 10–100 SKUs, and built around a small feature set with a free plan and one ¥99/month paid plan. It is currently in beta and does not try to match Shopify's ecosystem or WooCommerce's flexibility.

Dimension by dimension

Ease of use

Shopify is easy to start and grows more involved as apps accumulate. WooCommerce assumes WordPress familiarity. Senvarico is simple by design, with less to configure because there is less in it.

Technical maintenance

Shopify and Senvarico require no hosting or server management. WooCommerce requires ongoing maintenance, which is either the seller's time or a developer's invoice.

Catalog size

Shopify and WooCommerce handle catalogs of any size. Senvarico is designed for small catalogs and is not the right tool for hundreds or thousands of SKUs.

Budget

Pricing varies by plan on Shopify and is published on its official site. WooCommerce's core plugin is free but hosting, extensions and maintenance form the real cost. Senvarico has a Free plan (¥0/month, up to 20 SKUs) and a Small Seller plan (¥99/month, up to 100 SKUs), both with a 0% platform transaction fee.

Cross-border selling

Shopify supports international selling through its ecosystem. WooCommerce can do so through extensions. Senvarico includes cross-border basics in its core because its target seller usually sells across borders from the start.

Which seller should choose which

  • Choose Shopify if you expect to grow past a small catalog, need specific apps, or want the safest general-purpose choice.
  • Choose WooCommerce if you already use WordPress, want full control, and can handle or pay for maintenance.
  • Consider Senvarico if you are a solo cross-border seller or small team with a limited budget and a few dozen products, and you want a simple tool rather than a platform.

A small seller can reasonably start with Senvarico or Big Cartel and move to Shopify later, or start with Shopify and never move. What matters is choosing for the store you are running now.
